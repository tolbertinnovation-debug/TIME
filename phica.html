Our beginning

Where others saw an empty field, the founders saw classrooms.

Dr. Philip V. Saywrayne, III and Mrs. Tibeh E. Gbassagee-Saywrayne personally bought the land, an undeveloped field of savanna grass, and began building a school.

They imagined a place where children could sit in classrooms, hold books, learn to read and write, discover their abilities and begin to imagine a different future. That vision became Phìtìa Academy.

PHICA is more than a school building. It is a response to a need, an expression of hope, and a commitment to children whose circumstances should never determine the limits of their future.

The context

Children the Ebola crisis left behind

During and after the Ebola crisis, many children's education was interrupted for long periods. Some lost one or both parents and were raised by relatives, neighbors or the wider community. Others grew up in families already struggling with poverty.

Before PHICA, a community school was doing its best, but it lacked trained, qualified teachers and resources. At one point, three shipping containers were being used as classrooms, including for children orphaned by Ebola.

A shipping container can provide temporary shelter, but it cannot by itself provide the kind of educational environment every child deserves.

How PHICA works

Built on sacrifice, sustained by community

Founded on personal sacrifice

With no major donor or grant, the founders gave portions of their own salaries to pay teachers and keep the doors open.

Tuition-free for many

A substantial number of pupils attend tuition-free or receive significant financial help. Poverty should never be a permanent sentence on a child's future.

Community-supported

Families who can contribute pay US$60 or less (Nursery – Grade 6). Contributions from families who can afford them help keep education accessible to those who cannot.

Our mission

More than lessons: growing whole children

We want our pupils to grow academically, socially, morally and personally: to value health, hygiene, responsibility, cooperation, respect, integrity, compassion and service. A school close to home reduces the need for young children to make long, costly or unsafe journeys to learn.

  • Improving access to elementary education
  • Strengthening foundational literacy and numeracy
  • Supporting children from vulnerable households
  • Promoting health, hygiene and child well-being
  • Building community resilience after Ebola
  • Reducing educational inequality
Behind every need is a child

Where PHICA needs your help

PHICA still runs on limited resources without substantial external support. As enrollment grows, so do the needs.

A request for furniture means a child who deserves a proper place to sit. A request for textbooks means a child who should not learn without a book. A scholarship means a child who doesn't have to leave school because their family can't pay.

Our enduring principle

Every child deserves an opportunity to learn, regardless of family circumstances or economic status.

For us, this is not simply a statement. It is the reason the school exists. The founders began this journey with what they had. With the right partners, we can do much more together.
